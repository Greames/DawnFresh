### 1.3 Site-visit checklist (print one per premises)

Documents
- [ ] Ownership proof or authority of the lessor; property tax receipt up to date; building plan approval or occupancy status (UNVERIFIED whether municipal approval is demanded for the licence)
- [ ] Landlord NOC and draft lease with permitted use clause
- [ ] Electricity bill: existing connection category (domestic/commercial/industrial), sanctioned load, meter type; ask the lessor about arrears
- [ ] Previous use of the premises (a prior meat shop is a plus for neighbour tolerance)

Zoning and neighbours
- [ ] Talk to at least 3 adjacent shops/residents about odour, noise and vehicle movement
- [ ] Note religious places, schools, hospitals, and residences within about 50 m; ask the municipality whether any distance rule exists (UNVERIFIED)
- [ ] Prevailing wind direction relative to neighbours (odour)

Water
- [ ] Source (municipal/borewell/tanker), supply hours, pressure at tap
- [ ] Space for an overhead or sump tank of at least 2,000 L (processing needs roughly 20-30 L per kg of poultry dressed; ASSUMPTION, confirm with an equipment vendor)
- [ ] Take a sample for a lab test (potable standard per IS 10500); FSSAI inspections ask for a water report, typically renewed at least twice a year (UNVERIFIED frequency)
- [ ] Hardness/salinity taste check; borewell yield after summer (visit in dry season if possible)

Power
- [ ] 3-phase supply present? Distance to the nearest transformer; APSPDCL (Southern Power Distribution Company) new-connection or load-enhancement lead time (UNVERIFIED, ask APSPDCL Proddatur office)
- [ ] Wiring condition, earthing, space for a distribution board and generator changeover panel
- [ ] Outage history in the last 3 months (ask neighbours)
- [ ] Generator placement with exhaust and noise away from neighbours

Drainage and waste
- [ ] Floor slope toward the drain; outlet to a municipal drain, or soak pit and septic capacity
- [ ] Space to install a grease/blood trap and a screened floor drain
- [ ] Waste bin area with access from outside, not through the processing zone
- [ ] Check monsoon flooding history

Access
- [ ] Vehicle approach width, turning radius, gradient, parking for 2 vehicles
- [ ] Hours at which heavy vehicles are restricted in the area
- [ ] Separate entry for raw material in and finished goods out (or time-separated flow)

Building fabric
- [ ] Roof height of 3 m or more (cold room panels), ventilation, windows with insect screens
- [ ] Floor can be tiled and sloped; walls can be sealed to the height required (FSSAI audit material indicates about 1 m for poultry and small ruminants, higher in practice; check Schedule 4 text, UNVERIFIED)
- [ ] Pest ingress points; stray animal presence

FSSAI hygiene schedule items that the premises must allow (per FSSAI guidance summaries, confirm against the regulation text)
- [ ] Impervious, washable floors and walls; coved corners
- [ ] One-way flow from raw receiving to dispatch with no cross-over of raw and finished product
- [ ] Hand-wash basins with hot and cold water at the processing entrance and near toilets, with soap and drying provision
- [ ] Lighting around 540 lux at inspection points, 220 lux in work rooms, 110 lux elsewhere (as quoted in FSSAI audit material, UNVERIFIED for retail)
- [ ] Separate toilets and changing area for staff, not opening onto the processing zone
- [ ] Temperature-controlled storage with monitoring
- [ ] Waste handling and pest control provisions
- [ ] Meat sourced from a licensed/municipal slaughter facility or an FSSAI-licensed supplier

## 2. Recommended layout and zone plan

Concept: one building, two independent frontages. A walled processing and cold-storage unit at the back or on one side, and a separate retail counter with its own street entrance and its own hand-wash. No customer enters the processing unit.

Suggested area (ASSUMPTION): 800-1,000 sq ft processing/storage and 120-150 sq ft retail. If only one shop of about 600 sq ft is found, reduce the processing line to poultry dressing and boning only and buy cut-ready birds from a licensed processor (see open questions).

### 2.1 Zones (one-way flow, dirty to clean)

```
 Street (rear/side lane)                                   Street (front)
 [A] Receiving bay  ->  [B] Holding/chiller (raw)  ->  [C] Processing hall
       |                                                   |   (cut, debone, wash)
 [H] Waste bay (outside, separate door)                    v
                                                    [D] Weighing/packing/labelling
                                                           |
                                          [E] Cold room 0-4 C  |  [F] Freezer -18 C
                                                           |
                                    [G] Dispatch dock  ->  vehicle
                                                           |
                                          (pass window with a hatch)
                                                           v
                                         [R] Retail counter + display chiller
 Staff: [S] Entry, changing, lockers, toilets, hand-wash/boot-dip BEFORE [C]
```

Rules per zone
- A Receiving: washable floor, hose point, drain, weighing scale (for incoming), crates stacked off the floor. Clean crates only inside; dirty crates stay outside.
- B Raw holding: 0-4 C for dressed birds, mutton carcasses and fish on ice; must keep carcasses off the floor and not wrapped in wet cloth.
- C Processing: stainless steel or food-grade tables; floor drain with a trap; wall tiles to full working height (FSSAI references 1 m minimum for poultry; use 2 m or more); lighting at the stated lux; hot water point; knife sterilizer at about 82 C (typical practice, UNVERIFIED).
- D Packing: lowest-bacteria zone before storage; scale, label printer, vacuum sealer. Software link: weigh, label and scan here (see FRESHROUTE_BUSINESS_FLOW sections 6-7).
- E and F Cold storage: cold room for chilled; separate freezer for frozen. Fish and prawn on a separate shelf or a dedicated chest freezer to avoid odour and allergen cross-contact.
- G Dispatch: insulated door; vehicles load here, not through retail.
- R Retail: front counter with a glass display chiller (0-4 C) and chest freezer, hand-wash basin visible to customers, a separate chopping station for customer cutting (so open raw cutting does not happen in the processing hall), counter tiled at least to 1.5 m, bin, and Legal Metrology stamped scales.
- S Staff: changing room and toilets separate from the processing hall; hand-wash plus boot dip at the processing door; medical fitness records (FSSAI expectation).
- H Waste: closed bins, a separate outside door and a wash point; daily collection arrangement (B8).
- Separate small area for chemicals and a clean-equipment store.

Flow constraint: raw and cooked/finished never cross; people moving between R and C must change aprons.

## 3. Equipment list and sizing (pilot 150-300 kg/day)

### 3.1 Sizing assumptions (ASSUMPTIONS, test during dry run)

- Throughput design point 300 kg/day saleable meat; typical split (ASSUMPTION): chicken 60%, mutton 15%, fish/prawn 10%, eggs 15% by value (eggs counted in trays, not kg).
- Carry 1.5 days of stock (about 450 kg) to cover weekends and festivals; refrigerated volume rule of thumb of about 150-250 kg of boxed meat per cubic metre of cold room (UNVERIFIED rule of thumb; confirm with the supplier).
- Frozen stock about 25% of total (about 110 kg).
- Water use 20-30 L per kg dressed poultry (ASSUMPTION) so about 6,000-9,000 L/day if dressing 300 kg live-weight equivalent. If buying already dressed poultry this falls sharply.

### 6.3 Draft rule for choosing franchise territories

1. Anchor on a town centre or a high-density commercial point, not the franchisee's home. Record lat/lng to 5 decimals.
2. Default radius by setting (ASSUMPTION, adjust after pilot data):
   - Dense urban town (more than about 1 lakh population): 5-10 km.
   - Small town: 10-20 km (default 20).
   - Rural spread or sparse demand: 20-30 km, but only if daily road trips stay under about 60-70 minutes.
   - Never above 50 km (the software and Google Places limit).
3. Non-overlap: the distance between two franchise anchors must be at least the sum of their radii minus an agreed overlap allowance (suggest at most 20%); or the territory is split so no restaurant/hotel/caterer lead can belong to two franchisees. The software enforces one lead per Google place across the network, so overlaps cause disputes rather than duplicates.
4. Company-direct zone: the company territory (1-50 km) centred on the Proddatur unit; franchise territories should sit outside the core delivery area (about 10-12 km from the Proddatur unit) unless the franchise is explicitly company-licensed to operate within it. Proddatur town itself stays company-direct during the pilot, consistent with the plan's risk row (run a company-owned pilot first).
5. Supply-chain test: the franchise must be reachable from the Proddatur unit within the shelf-life window: chilled raw meat delivered within about 2-3 hours by insulated vehicle, or within about 4-6 hours with a reefer. Under this test, Jammalamadugu and Mydukur qualify for insulated delivery; Kadapa (about 44-54 km road) needs a reefer or hired transport.
6. Demand test (before assigning): at least about 30 restaurants/caterers/hotels within the radius (use the app's territory discovery, max 20 results per search, so run separate searches per type; do not call a result list exhaustive), plus population and income checks.
7. Regulatory test: the franchisee's premises must hold its own FSSAI registration/licence, trade licence and meet the same hygiene schedule; add a pre-appointment inspection checklist (reuse section 1).
8. Review: after 90 days, recompute radius from actual delivery times, order density and complaints; the owner changes radius in the Franchises form (admin).
