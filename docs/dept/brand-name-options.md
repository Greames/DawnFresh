## 1. How to check availability in India

Do all checks for the shortlisted name before paying for signage, FSSAI labels or domain renewals. Run them in this order, cheapest first.

1. **Google and marketplaces (free, 15 min).** Search the exact name and spelling variants in English and Telugu transliteration, with words "chicken", "meat", "fish", "eggs", "fresh", "mart". Check Google Maps for Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, plus Justdial, IndiaMART, Swiggy/Zomato. Note any business using a similar name in food, retail or logistics.
2. **IP India trademark public search.** Portal: ipindiaonline.gov.in/tmrpublicsearch (source: intepat.com guide). Choose Wordmark and search one class at a time (the database allows only a single class per search, per the same guide). Run each name in:
   - Class 29 (meat, fish, poultry, eggs, processed/frozen meat).
   - Class 35 (retail, wholesale and distribution services, franchising business support).
   - Class 43 only if you plan a restaurant, cloud kitchen or catering link.
   - Class 39 if you brand a delivery/transport service (general knowledge, UNVERIFIED).
   Also run the **Phonetic** search type (finds sound-alike names) and search spelling variants (for example Fresh/Frsh/Fresha, Dawn/Dawne, Trail/Trayl). Search also for the same words with a space, with a hyphen, and with a Telugu/Devanagari rendering. Check status of hits: registered, pending, objected, abandoned. A confusingly similar mark in a related class is a refusal or opposition risk even if the exact name is free. Trademark Registry search should be repeated by a trademark attorney immediately before filing.
   Note: "Fresh" is a descriptive word for food. Marks built on it are usually weak and may need a distinctive logo or word element; the attorney should advise (general knowledge, UNVERIFIED).
3. **MCA name check (only if you incorporate a company or LLP).** Company names are reserved through SPICe+ Part A and LLP names through RUN-LLP (consultancy blogs, quicko/registerkaro; UNVERIFIED against MCA). The name must not be identical or too similar to an existing company, LLP or trademark; MCA name approval is not trademark protection and a name can pass MCA and still infringe a mark. Fee and validity figures varied between sources (company about Rs 1,000; LLP about Rs 200; validity 20 or 60 days), and sources describe proposed 2026 changes to Rules 8 and 8A that I could not confirm as final. Check the current rules on mca.gov.in. Searching MCA's "Check Company Name" tool before submission is free (general knowledge, UNVERIFIED). A legal entity name can differ from the brand name, but the trademark owner should be the entity that will franchise.
4. **Domain.** Check .in and .com on a registrar (for example any ICANN accredited registrar or the NIXI-accredited .in registrars). Buy both if possible; also check common misspellings. Do not rely on a WHOIS "available" result as a legal clearance.
5. **Social handles.** Check Instagram, Facebook page name, YouTube handle (@name), X, WhatsApp Business display name, and Google Business Profile name search. Aim for the same handle everywhere.
6. **Telugu and Hindi sense check.** Ask 3-5 Telugu speakers (Proddatur locals and a Hindi speaker) to say the name aloud and write it in Telugu script. Check it does not sound like an offensive or comic word. I cannot verify meanings in Telugu; all Telugu/Hindi notes below are general knowledge, UNVERIFIED.
7. **Then file.** Once checked, the owner/CA files the trademark application (see `docs/dept/registrations.md`, A13) before signage and label printing.

## 1. Surname brands in Indian food

- **Evidence found:** Only one example was researched. ## 2. Trademark treatment of surnames in India (UNVERIFIED, for the attorney)

- A commentary says the old 1958 Act expressly barred surname registration, while the Trade Marks Act 1999 does not contain that limit (ssrana.in, mondaq.com).
- Names count as marks (Section 2(1)(m)). The main hurdle is Section 9(1)(a) (distinctive character). A common surname can draw an objection that it is a common surname or personal name. A 2026 Delhi High Court decision (ADS Spirits v. Registrar of Trade Marks, reported by verdictum.in and theindianlawyer.in) held that Section 9(1)(a) tests distinctive character in relation to the goods, not "uniqueness". The mark in that case was a common surname-like word, but I have not read the judgment.
- Acquired distinctiveness through use can overcome an objection (Section 9(1) proviso, per the same commentary), and honest concurrent use can be registered (Section 12). The combined mark "VelamFresh" with a rooster logo is more likely to be distinctive than "Velam" alone, because "Fresh" is descriptive and weak (attorney to advise on whether to disclaim "Fresh" and on logo versus wordmark filing).
- **Unconfirmed in this search:** how Registry examiners treat a rarely used surname versus a common one, Section 11 conflicts with other Velam marks, and any consent requirement for a personal name. Ask the attorney for a written opinion.

## 3. Franchising, ownership and operator handover

- India has no standalone franchise statute; franchising rests on contract, competition, consumer and IP law, and the trademark licence falls under the Trade Marks Act 1999 (atblegal.com, iiprd.com, incorpx.io). The franchisor should own the brand and control quality; failure to control use risks "naked licensing" (same sources). Sources conflict on registered-user and licence registration sections and on a six-month deadline: attorney to confirm.
- **For a surname brand, decide now who owns the mark.** Options for the attorney and CA to compare: (a) the business entity (LLP/Pvt Ltd) owns the trademark, and the owner as individual assigns any prior rights in the name to it; (b) the owner personally holds the mark and licenses it to the entity and franchisees. Option (a) makes sale or handover simpler; option (b) keeps control with the owner but complicates financing, partners and operator takeover. Which is better depends on tax, control and exit plans, not covered here.
- **If an operator takes over:** the agreement must state whether the operator buys the business including the name (assignment), only licenses it for a term, or must rebrand on exit. If the name is the owner's surname, the owner may not want it on a business they no longer control; a neutral coined name avoids this problem and is easier to sell. Also address: use of the owner's personal name in advertising, owner's personal liability image if the operator fails food safety, non-compete, and whether the owner may use the surname in a future business.
- **Franchisees and customers outside AP/Telangana** will see an unfamiliar word; inside the Telugu states, the name will be read as a surname.
